5. Conclusion

JWST has revealed a coherent pattern of anomalies at $z > 5$: ultra-massive galaxies with star formation efficiencies exceeding $\Lambda$CDM limits and stellar masses that can exceed dynamical masses. What links these anomalies is not merely that they are surprising, but that they cluster in the deepest gravitational potentials and point in the same direction — photometrically inferred stellar properties appear too large and too early. This work tested whether a single violation of the isochrony axiom, encoded by the continuously screened Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP), can account for that shared structure. Using the prespecified canonical TEP benchmark $\kappa_{\rm gal} = (9.6 \pm 4.0) \times 10^5$ mag from Paper 11 with no JWST retuning, the framework reproduces the scale of the Red Monster efficiency excess and brings the $M_*/M_{\rm dyn}$ anomaly to the admissibility boundary in the direct object-level comparison (mean corrected excess $+0.015$ dex on the anomalous subset at the canonical benchmark; two of three anomalous objects resolved). The canonical benchmark exceeds the empirical Cepheid-channel recovery by a factor $\sim 2.4$ — an open response-normalization residual attributed to the undetermined transfer product $S_{\rm Cep}\Gamma_{\rm Cep}$, stated explicitly and propagated through the prior range rather than fitted to the high-redshift data. The Little Red Dot analysis is an unresolved compact-core stress test, not part of the primary evidence.

5.1 Synthesis of Results

The core empirical case rests on the dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ emergence (L1) as the primary photometric line, supported by a secondary $R_{\rm ML}$–sSFR partial correlation line (L3). The SUSPENSE kinematic comparison (L5) narrows the mass-circularity objection: $R_{\rm ML}$ retains residual age information after $M_*$+$z$ control ($\rho = +0.556$, $p=0.032$), whereas $M_*$ contributes no residual signal once $R_{\rm ML}$+$z$ are controlled — a suggestive one-sided asymmetry at $N=15$ whose direct predictor comparison remains non-significant (Steiger $p=0.253$), pending replication. A broader ($N=75$) sigma-based expansion is mixed and construction-sensitive: the pooled residual-$z$ trend is positive in stored convention ($\rho=+0.46$, $p=3\times10^{-5}$) but is a between-survey composition effect, since within each of the four testable surveys the conventional trend is instead weakly positive and individually non-significant. The absorption-line subsample ($N=20$, the designated primary tracer) is opposite-signed only under a $\sigma$-to-$M_{\rm halo}$ map that embeds expanding-cosmology virial drift; under a cosmology-free $\sigma$-to-depth map the absorption and SUSPENSE subsamples carry the predicted sign and the TEP mass correction attenuates their trends toward zero in the predicted direction. The sigma-only $R_{\rm ML}$ secondary partial is null ($\rho=+0.079$, $p=0.50$), though intrinsically low-power as a deterministic function of the conditioned variables. Within the photometric sample itself, the circularity-breaker battery (§3.4.1) supplies functional-form discrimination: the canonical $R_{\rm ML}$ outperforms a power-law placebo ($\Delta\rho = +0.099$, 95% CI $[+0.028, +0.174]$), and the narrow-mass-bin correlations execute the kernel-predicted negative-to-positive sign transition (4/4 bins, crossover at $\log M_* \simeq 9.6$) that no uniform-sign monotone-in-$z$ baseline can produce. L2 provides specific controlled central-concentration support (Gini $\rho=+0.191$), though the direct colour-gradient partial is null ($\rho=+0.037$, $p=0.54$) — a result that may reflect a predictor mismatch, since the global halo-scale $R_{\rm ML}$ cannot resolve the radial screening profile. L4 provides a derived dynamical-mass comparison that lands the anomalous literature objects at the admissibility boundary at the canonical benchmark.

5.2 Interpretative Framework

Physical processes require proper time. Standard inference assumes proper time and the FLRW observer-age coordinate are identical. The JWST anomalies appear precisely where this identification fails. In the canonical TEP cosmology the spatial manifold is static and the gravitational coordinate time extends without finite origin (TEP-HUB, Paper 30); the FLRW age $t_{\rm cosmic}(z)$ is a reconstruction under isochrony that shrinks to a few hundred Myr at high redshift. Local clocks run slower than coordinate time ($\Delta\ln A<0$ in a deeper well), but the coordinate background is eternal, so accumulated proper time can still exceed the FLRW assigned age by a large factor. The inference-channel response $R_{\rm ML}$ quantifies how much the standard pipeline inflates apparent age and $M/L$ when it uses the too-short FLRW baseline, and a single parameter propagates coherently through stellar ages, mass-to-light ratios, apparent dust-evolution diagnostics, star-formation diagnostics, and dynamical-mass comparisons.

A nested Bayesian model comparison using a joint covariance likelihood that accounts for correlated SED outputs is conditioning-dependent: $\ln{\rm BF}=+41.5$ under orthogonalized predictors (and $+37.4$ in the 3-observable physical subset) versus $\ln{\rm BF}=-14.4$ under the raw mass+z baseline ($-20.4$ in the subset). The flip is structural rather than a bug: $\log R_{\rm ML}$ is a deterministic function of (mass, z) (${\rm corr}=0.86$, $R^2=0.74$), so orthogonalizing mass strips the comparator of the signal direction. The nested augmented model shows the predictor families mutually subsumed ($\Delta\ln Z = +18.9$ for adding mass+z to $R_{\rm ML}$), and a direction-isolation diagnostic assigns $55.9$ ln units to the $R_{\rm ML}$-aligned direction of mass space — the variance is concentrated in the direction the theory specifies. A free mass–redshift interaction is the strongest baseline ($\ln Z=-1190.9$, $+57.3$ over raw), and adding the instantiated kernel on top contributes $-0.9$. The ambient-field proxy audit (step\_182) initially appears to supply the missing direction ($+16.2$ beyond mass+z, $z$-independent), but the completed audit resolves it as a systematic: Abell 2744 is a foreground cluster ($z = 0.308$) that cannot belong to a $z>8$ galaxy's ambient field, and the clustercentric coordinate's predictive power is the lensing-magnification/mosaic-depth signature — confirmed by the DR4 lens model, ${\rm corr}(\log\mu_{\rm ML}, d_{\rm A2744})=-0.82$ and ${\rm corr}(\log\mu_{\rm ML},\chi^2)=+0.44$. With $\{M_*,z,\mu,d_{\rm A2744}\}$ in the null baseline ($+34.2$ over mass+z), the theory-faithful mass-weighted same-epoch density proxy contributes $-7.1$ (full) and $-6.0$ (physical subset): the environmental-channel evidence is fully explained by the foreground-lens systematics, and functional-form discrimination of the ambient-field law is not achievable with this field's selection geometry. The Bayesian comparison is accordingly read as a conditioning-corrected control result — the $R_{\rm ML}$-aligned direction organizes the explanatory variance, but neither the instantiated kernel nor a same-epoch ambient proxy supplies unique evidence beyond the systematics-controlled baseline — and the supportive weight of the analysis rests on the kinematic comparisons (SUSPENSE) and the multi-survey photometric correlation structure.

The cross-domain consistency of the coupling remains a major feature of the evidence base. The prespecified canonical benchmark $\kappa_{\rm gal} = (9.6 \pm 4.0) \times 10^5$ mag from Paper 11 (where empirical checks recover $(0.369 \pm 0.310)\times 10^6$ and $(0.400 \pm 0.270)\times 10^6$ mag) provides the magnitude-sector normalization, while the regenerated informative JWST high-redshift analyses recover $\kappa = (8.38 \pm 4.11) \times 10^5$ mag. This is consistent with the canonical benchmark at $0.21\sigma$ and equally within $\sim 1.1\sigma$ of the empirical Cepheid recoveries, so the concordance test ($p_{\rm concordance}=1.0$) confirms anchor consistency without discriminating between the two anchors. The factor $\sim 2.4$ offset between the canonical benchmark and the empirical recoveries stands as an open normalization discrepancy of the corpus rather than a resolved agreement.

Key signatures survive a 0.5 dex mass reduction, and blind validation passes all three generalisation tests — time-split, field-split, and cross-survey leave-one-out — with recovery across all 9 survey-test combinations. Each of the three independent JWST surveys confirms the dust relation individually above $5\sigma$, and all three independently confirm that $t_{\rm eff}$ outperforms $t_{\rm cosmic}$ at $>5\sigma$ (combined Steiger $Z \approx 25.8$), ruling out pure redshift ordering. A Fisher combination across the three photometric datasets gives $z = 25.1\sigma$ ($p = 3.0 \times 10^{-139}$). Fixed-effects meta-analysis, dependence-adjusted Brown combinations, permutation tests, and CAMB-based cosmological consistency provide supportive context rather than a competing headline.

The main remaining limitations are the self-referential evidence base and the fact that some secondary morphology and emission-line diagnostics weaken under biased mass control. These caveats sit within an evidential structure whose spine is coherent: the primary photometric line (L1), a secondary $R_{\rm ML}$–sSFR partial correlation line (L3), a direct kinematic comparison whose one-sided conditional asymmetry narrows the mass-circularity objection, an ancillary screening indication, and a derived dynamical-mass comparison.
