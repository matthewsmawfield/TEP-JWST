3.1 Red Monsters: A No-JWST-Specific-Refit Prediction

The TEP parameterization is applied to galaxies in the Red Monster regime ($z \sim 5$–$6$, $\log M_* \gtrsim 10.5$; Xiao et al. 2024). This is a predictive test: the prespecified canonical TEP magnitude-sector benchmark $\kappa_{\rm gal} = (9.6 \pm 4.0) \times 10^5$ mag from Paper 11 was fixed prior to the high-redshift fit (with Paper 11's independent empirical Cepheid analyses recovering $\kappa_{\rm equiv}^{42} = (0.369 \pm 0.310) \times 10^6$ mag and $\kappa_{\rm Cep}^{44} = (0.400 \pm 0.270) \times 10^6$ mag). The regenerated high-redshift concordance analysis recovers $\kappa = (8.38 \pm 4.11) \times 10^5$ mag from the informative analyses, internally concordant ($p_{\rm concordance}=1.0$) and consistent with the canonical benchmark at $0.21\sigma$ — and equally within $\sim 1.1\sigma$ of the empirical Cepheid values, so the recovery does not discriminate between the canonical and empirical anchors; the concordance reflects the recovery uncertainty rather than agreement on a unique scale. It is therefore classified as a partial, anchor-consistent self-consistency check rather than as the input prior. No parameters are fitted or tuned to the high-redshift observations. The three entries below (S1–S3) use representative parameters spanning the published range (§2.1.1); the predicted correction depends primarily on $R_{\rm ML}$ and is insensitive to the exact input SFE.

Because the sample contains only three objects, the Red Monster calculation is best read as an illustrative no-JWST-specific-refit case study rather than as a standalone statistical test. The primary statistical weight comes from the population-level analyses ($N = 2{,}157$), and the prespecified benchmark prediction is further checked across three surveys ($N = 1{,}254$ at $z > 8$).

3.2 UNCOVER DR4: Mass-sSFR and Mass-Age Correlations

The Red Monster case study establishes that TEP predicts the correct direction and magnitude of the SFE correction for individual extreme objects. The critical question is whether this signal extends to the full galaxy population. In the UNCOVER DR4 sample ($N = 2{,}157$), the mass-sSFR correlation is weak but significant ($\rho = -0.14$, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-11}$, Cohen's $d = -0.29$). Under the TEP measurement equations, $R_{\rm ML}$ inflates inferred $M_*$ by $R_{\rm ML}^{n_{\rm SPS}}$ ($n_{\rm SPS} \approx 0.7$) and inflates inferred SFR by $R_{\rm ML}^m$ ($m \approx 0.5$), so inferred sSFR $= {\rm SFR}/M_*$ scales as $R_{\rm ML}^{m-n_{\rm SPS}}$. Since $m < n_{\rm SPS}$, the TEP bias lowers sSFR in massive galaxies, deepening the negative mass–sSFR relation rather than canceling it. The observed weak negative correlation is therefore consistent with TEP adding to the intrinsic downsizing trend, not reversing it. The mass-age correlation is positive ($\rho = +0.15$, $p = 2.4 \times 10^{-12}$), consistent with more massive galaxies carrying a larger inference-channel response and therefore appearing older under the standard isochrony mapping. Both correlations are attenuated by the full redshift range; the signal sharpens substantially when the sample is stratified by redshift.

3.3 Redshift Evolution: The High-z Transition

The TEP measurement equations predict $\rho(R_{\rm ML}, {\rm sSFR}) < 0$ at all redshifts, since ${\rm sSFR}_{\rm obs} \propto R_{\rm ML}^{m-n_{\rm SPS}}$ with $m < n_{\rm SPS}$. This is confirmed: the full-sample $R_{\rm ML}$–sSFR correlation yields $\rho = -0.49$ ($p = 7.7 \times 10^{-129}$, $N=2{,}157$), and the partial correlation controlling for $M_*$ and $z$ gives $\rho(R_{\rm ML}, {\rm sSFR} \mid M_*, z) = -0.62$ ($p = 8.2 \times 10^{-29}$, $N=254$) at $z > 8$. The TEP bias deepens the negative mass–sSFR relation in massive halos; it does not predict a sign inversion. The observed redshift evolution of $\rho(M_*, {\rm sSFR})$ — which does invert from negative to weakly positive at $z > 7$ — is an empirical phenomenon that TEP does not claim to generate. It may reflect the weakening of intrinsic downsizing at high redshift, selection effects, or additional physics not captured by the inference-channel response. 3.4 Partial Correlation Test

The redshift evolution in §3.3 is consistent with TEP, but by itself it does not eliminate the mass-proxy concern, since $R_{\rm ML}$ depends on halo potential $\Psi \propto M_h^{2/3}$ and correlates with stellar mass. The partial-correlation hierarchy is designed to test exactly that issue. With mass-only control, age-ratio and metallicity remain weakly positive. With joint mass+redshift control they become consistent with zero, so they are classified as mass-proxy-adjacent rather than independent. The high-redshift dust signal behaves differently: at $z > 8$, the dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ correlation survives joint mass+redshift control ($\rho = +0.257$, $p = 1.2 \times 10^{-5}$, Cohen's $d = +0.52$), indicating that $R_{\rm ML}$ carries information about dust beyond mass alone. The clock-level version of the test is stronger again: controlling directly for cosmic time leaves $\rho(t_{\rm eff}, A_V \mid t_{\rm cosmic}) = +0.472$ ($p = 5.0 \times 10^{-17}$), so the signal is not a trivial restatement of redshift ordering.

The key asymmetry: the SUSPENSE kinematic comparison (§3.10) provides the cleanest test. $R_{\rm ML}$ retains residual age information after $M_*$+$z$ control ($\rho = +0.556$, $p = 0.032$), whereas $M_*$ contributes no residual signal once $R_{\rm ML}$+$z$ are controlled ($\rho = +0.031$, $p = 0.91$). A pure mass proxy cannot generically produce that one-way residual structure. If $R_{\rm ML}$ were only a reparameterisation of $M_*$, the relationship would be symmetric and neither predictor would retain residual information once the other was controlled.

A further complication is that if TEP is correct, SED-inferred stellar masses are themselves biased upward by $R_{\rm ML}^{0.7}$. Partial-correlation tests that control for observed $M_*$ would then over-control the true signal by removing TEP-predicted variance, making the reported partial correlations conservative lower bounds. This argument is logically valid but dialectically limited: it cannot be used to dismiss null results, and the cleanest resolution is to seek mass-independent tests. The circularity-breaker tests in §3.4.1 and the dynamical-mass comparison in §3.10 provide that independent route.

Mass-range sensitivity: the partial correlation $\rho(R_{\rm ML}, A_V \mid M_*, z) = +0.31$ is concentrated in the high-mass tail where $R_{\rm ML}$ transitions from screened ($R_{\rm ML} < 1$) to unscreened ($R_{\rm ML} > 1$) values. Restricting to $\log M_* < 9.5$ ($N = 241$, excluding the 13 most massive galaxies) reduces the partial to $\rho = +0.27$ ($p = 2.4 \times 10^{-5}$), and restricting to $\log M_* < 9$ ($N = 228$) gives $\rho = +0.24$ ($p = 3.5 \times 10^{-4}$). The raw mass–dust correlation, by contrast, remains robust at $\rho = +0.49$ ($p = 6.8 \times 10^{-15}$) even for $\log M_* < 9$. This means the TEP-specific residual beyond mass+$z$ is driven by the non-linear $R_{\rm ML}$ transition at the high-mass end, not by a universal linear correlation. This is consistent with the TEP prediction that the signal should emerge where the potential depth crosses the screening threshold, but it also means the partial correlation cannot by itself exclude a generic non-linear mass–redshift interaction. The Steiger comparison (§3.7.5) and the Bayesian model comparison (§3.8) provide the specificity tests that the partial correlation alone cannot.

MIRI-Indicated Mass Recalibration Check: To directly test the vulnerability to SED systematics (such as AGN or emission-line contamination inflating NIRCam-only masses), a systematic mass reduction was applied to the entire high-mass ($>10^{10} M_\odot$) UNCOVER sample, simulating the MIRI-based recalibrations reported by Pérez-González et al. (2024). When all masses are artificially reduced by 0.5 dex, the $R_{\rm ML}$-dust correlation at $z > 8$ remains significant ($\rho = +0.447$, $p = 2.8 \times 10^{-15}$). The signal survives even a full 1.0 dex systematic reduction ($\rho = +0.310$, $p = 9.9 \times 10^{-8}$). The correlation weakens under mass reduction — expected because $R_{\rm ML}$ is a non-linear function of halo mass — but remains statistically significant even under extreme calibration shifts, confirming that the TEP signal is not an artifact of the absolute photometric mass calibration.

3.4.1 Circular Mass-Loop Breaker Tests

The most fundamental structural criticism of the photometric analysis is the circular mass loop: $R_{\rm ML}$ is computed from halo mass, halo mass is estimated from stellar mass via abundance matching, and TEP claims stellar mass is itself biased by $R_{\rm ML}^n$. The $N = 2{,}157$ sample is caught in this loop; only the SUSPENSE kinematic test ($N = 15$; §3.10) fully escapes it. Three additional tests directly target this circularity on the full photometric sample.

Test A — 2D mass-$z$ pairing shuffle: Rather than shuffling mass within narrow $z$-bins (as in step 143, which preserves the $z$-dependence of $R_{\rm ML}$), this test randomly reassigns each galaxy's stellar mass to a different galaxy's redshift, breaking both the mass-$z$ correlation and the $R_{\rm ML}$ functional form simultaneously. At $z > 8$ ($N = 254$), the observed $\rho(R_{\rm ML}, A_V) = +0.646$ collapses to a shuffled mean of $-0.002 \pm 0.055$ ($Z = 10.9$, $p = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ over 2,000 shuffles). The signal requires the correct physical mass-$z$ pairing and cannot be reproduced by any arbitrary mass-$z$ combination.

Test B — Placebo $R_{\rm ML}$ (wrong functional form): A placebo predictor with the same mass and redshift dependence but a different functional form — a power-law $(M_h / M_{h,{\rm ref}})^{0.3} (1+z)^{0.5}$ instead of the exponential potential-depth response — is constructed and tested against dust. Evaluated with the canonical bounded, self-consistent $R_{\rm ML}$ used throughout the pipeline, the real response yields $\rho = +0.646$ and the placebo yields $\rho = +0.507$. The bootstrap difference is $\Delta\rho = +0.139$ with 95% CI $[+0.065, +0.213]$: the real response is superior in 99.3% of 2,000 bootstrap draws and the interval excludes zero, so the exponential potential-depth form is statistically distinguished from a generic power-law mass-$z$ interaction on the photometric sample alone. An earlier non-decisive version of this comparison used a non-canonical, unbounded response construction inconsistent with the pipeline's calibrated variable; once the same bounded self-consistent $R_{\rm ML}$ is used for both predictors, the advantage of the TEP form is resolved at the stated confidence.

Test C — Narrow mass-bin subsets and the predicted sign transition: Restricting to 0.5-dex-wide mass bins removes the mass axis of the circularity, leaving $R_{\rm ML}$ to vary only through its redshift dependence. The reference-depth kernel makes a falsifiable sign prediction here: within a bin, $\mathrm{d}\ln R_{\rm ML}/\mathrm{d}z = K_{\rm exp}\sqrt{1+z}\,\mathrm{d}\Psi/\mathrm{d}z + K_{\rm exp}(\Psi - \Psi_{\rm ref})/(2\sqrt{1+z})$ changes sign across a crossover mass set by the competition between the positive stellar-to-halo-mass drift and the $(\Psi - \Psi_{\rm ref})$ term. The observed within-bin correlations execute exactly this transition: $\log M_* \in [8.0, 8.5]$ ($N = 1{,}528$, $\rho = -0.346$, $p = 2.9 \times 10^{-44}$), $\log M_* \in [8.5, 9.0]$ ($N = 526$, $\rho = -0.457$, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-28}$), $\log M_* \in [9.0, 9.5]$ ($N = 190$, $\rho = -0.023$, consistent with zero at the crossover), and $\log M_* \in [9.5, 10.0]$ ($N = 48$, $\rho = +0.347$, $p = 0.016$). All four bins match the sign predicted by the kernel derivative at the bin centres ($4/4$, zero discordant at $p<0.05$), with the interpolated crossover at $\log M_* \simeq 9.6$ ($\log M_h \simeq 11.3$, $\Psi \simeq 0.35\,\Psi_{\rm ref}$, shifted below the reference depth by the positive within-bin SMHM drift).

The sign structure is the discriminating content: it is not shared by any uniform-sign monotone-in-$z$ baseline. Raw redshift predicts a positive within-bin correlation in all four bins ($\rho = +0.36$, $+0.50$, $+0.19$, $+0.44$), the power-law placebo likewise ($\rho = +0.28$, $+0.43$, $+0.32$, $+0.45$), and a deliberately inverted monotone baseline $M_*^{0.3}(1+z)^{-0.5}$ predicts a negative correlation in all four ($\rho = -0.29$, $-0.40$, $-0.04$, $-0.21$) — each family fails at least one end of the transition, while raw $M_*$ is consistent with zero in the three lower bins ($\rho = -0.03$, $-0.01$, $+0.15$) and cannot generate the negative branch at all. Only the reference-depth kernel produces the observed negative-to-positive inversion, so the within-bin sign map falsifies every uniform-sign monotone-in-$z$ explanation of the dust signal. The negative sign in the two largest bins is therefore not the ordinary dust–redshift trend read backwards: dust rises with $z$ inside those bins, yet $R_{\rm ML}$ orders anti-correlated with $z$ precisely where $\Psi < \Psi_{\rm ref}$ — the signature of a response anchored to a reference potential depth, not of a generic mass-$z$ correlation.

Assessment: 3 of 3 circularity-breaking tests pass. The 2D shuffle confirms the signal requires the correct mass-$z$ pairing; the placebo comparison now resolves the exponential potential-depth form over a generic power-law ($\Delta\rho$ CI excludes zero); and the narrow-mass-bin sign map reproduces the kernel-predicted crossover that no monotone-in-$z$ baseline can generate. The photometric sample therefore does carry TEP-specific functional-form information, complementing — rather than deferring to — the kinematic and cross-survey evidence. The remaining limitations are quantitative: the positive-sign bin is supported by only $N = 48$ galaxies, and ordinary linear-model AIC comparison (step 136) finds the $M_* \times z$ interaction and the TEP predictor comparably efficient ($\Delta$AIC $\simeq 1$–$2$), so the sign-structure and placebo tests — not regression ranking — carry the discrimination.

3.6 The z > 8 Dust Anomaly: Correlation Structure

The $R_{\rm ML}$–sSFR partial correlation (§3.3) and the partial-correlation hierarchy (§3.4) show that $R_{\rm ML}$ carries information beyond a simple mass trend. The clearest empirical test is the dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ correlation structure. Under standard physics, the universe at $z \sim 9$ is only $\sim 540$ Myr old, barely enough for the first generation of AGB stars to complete their evolution. The question is not whether the dust budget can be closed under TEP — the inferred-time proxy $t_{\rm eff} = R_{\rm ML} \, t_{\rm cosmic}$ is not a physical proper-time integral (§2.3.2) and cannot be used to compute physical AGB evolution timescales. The question is whether the pattern of dust emergence across the galaxy population is organized by the inference-channel response $R_{\rm ML}$ more strongly than by the simple time-only and mass-only baselines tested here.

The Uniformity Paradox ($N=33$ massive galaxies at $z > 8$)

Under standard physics, the cosmic time $t_{\rm cosmic}(z)$ available for dust production is uniform across all galaxies at a given redshift. If dust parameters (AGB yield, SN yield, destruction timescale) are tuned to close the budget at $z > 8$, dust should be ubiquitous or track star formation. Instead, observations reveal a strong mass-dependent suppression ($\rho = +0.56$): massive galaxies are dusty; low-mass galaxies are dust-poor. A purely redshift- or time-only explanation cannot reproduce the observed mass-dependent gradient. Under TEP, this gradient arises because the inference-channel response $R_{\rm ML}$ orders the apparent evolutionary advance with potential depth: massive halos ($R_{\rm ML} > 1$) appear more evolved and dust-rich, while low-mass halos ($R_{\rm ML} \ll 1$) appear less evolved and dust-poor. The anomaly is not that massive galaxies have dust — it is that low-mass galaxies do not, in a pattern that tracks gravitational potential depth rather than star formation rate or cosmic time.

Recent JWST spectroscopy shows that AGB stars produce SiC and iron dust even at low metallicity ($\sim 1$–$7\%\,Z_\odot$; Boyer et al. 2025), with onset as early as 30–50 Myr for the most massive AGB progenitors. The physical question of whether the canonical TEP matter-frame proper time $\tau_\star = \int A[\phi(t, \mathbf{x})] \, dt$ is sufficient for AGB evolution at high redshift is left for future work; the present analysis establishes the empirical correlation structure, not the physical dust-budget closure.

3.6.1 The $z = 5$–$7$ Dip

The redshift-binned correlations reveal a more complex pattern than a simple monotonic emergence. At $z = 5$–$6$, $\rho(A_V, R_{\rm ML}) = -0.14$ ($p = 1.2 \times 10^{-3}$) and $\rho(A_V, t_{\rm eff}) = -0.19$ ($p = 3.3 \times 10^{-5}$), both significantly negative — the opposite sign from the TEP-predicted positive correlation. At $z = 6$–$7$, $\rho(A_V, R_{\rm ML}) = -0.04$ ($p = 0.47$, non-significant). The signal only turns positive and significant at $z > 8$: $\rho(A_V, R_{\rm ML}) = +0.36$ ($p = 6.7 \times 10^{-7}$) at $z = 8$–$9$ and $\rho = +0.76$ ($p = 9.8 \times 10^{-21}$) at $z = 9$–$10$. The negative correlations at $z = 5$–$7$ are a genuine anomaly for the universal-correlation interpretation of TEP: if $R_{\rm ML}$ universally enhances dust production, the correlation should be positive at all redshifts. The "competition epoch" interpretation — high sSFR depleting dust faster than AGB stars replenish it — is a post-hoc explanation, not a prespecified prediction. This anomaly is reported transparently: the TEP signal is an emergence phenomenon at $z > 8$, not a universal correlation, and the intermediate-redshift negative correlations are currently unexplained by the theory.

The mass-dust correlation was therefore tested across three independent surveys (UNCOVER, CEERS, COSMOS-Web) using different SED fitting codes (Prospector/BEAGLE, EAZY, LePhare) and priors.

3.7.2 Meta-Analysis

Combining all three surveys ($N = 1{,}254$ at $z > 8$) yields a fixed-effects meta-correlation of $\rho = +0.635$ with $p = 3.65 \times 10^{-154}$ (Cohen's $d = 1.63$, a large effect). All three surveys independently confirm the positive $\rho$ at $>6\sigma$, and mass-stratification confirms the signal persists at fixed mass. The per-survey effect sizes are $\rho = +0.65$ (UNCOVER, $N = 254$), $\rho = +0.66$ (CEERS, $N = 82$), and $\rho = +0.63$ (COSMOS-Web, $N = 918$). Between-study heterogeneity is negligible ($I^2 = 0\%$, Cochran's $Q = 0.24$, $p_Q = 0.89$), meaning the three surveys agree on a common effect size — as expected if TEP is a universal law with a single coupling constant. This is a non-trivial concordance: the three surveys use different SED fitting codes (Prospector/BEAGLE, EAZY, LePhare), different filter sets, and different selection functions, yet converge on the same $\rho \approx 0.64$. Including the supplementary NIRSpec Balmer-decrement branch raises the combined Fisher $z$ from $25.1\sigma$ to $26.2\sigma$, while the Balmer partial-$\rho$ branch itself is weak after $M_*$+$z$ control ($\rho = 0.136$, $p = 5.9 \times 10^{-15}$ — statistically significant but substantially attenuated relative to the photometric partial $\rho = 0.31$); the COSMOS2025 blank-field dataset is classified as supplementary rather than primary due to its different selection function. A lensing-systematic control specific to the UNCOVER field further isolates the correlation: restricting to unmagnified sources ($\mu_{\rm ML} \le 1.05$, $N = 144$, DR4 v2.0 lens model) leaves $\rho = +0.585$ ($p = 1.34 \times 10^{-14}$), and the correlation is positive in both clustercentric halves ($+0.667$ inner, $+0.580$ outer), with $\rho(\mu_{\rm ML}, A_V) = -0.061$ ($p = 0.33$) — the dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ structure is not carried by the lensing gradient, in contrast to the environmental-density channels audited in §3.8. The magnification-corrected mass diagnostic confirms this: evaluating $R_{\rm ML}$ with $\mu$-corrected stellar masses ($\log M_{*,\rm true} = \log M_{*,\rm obs} - \log_{10}\mu$) strengthens the correlation to $\rho = +0.656$ ($p = 1.30 \times 10^{-32}$, $N = 254$; $\rho = +0.603$ at $\mu \le 1.05$), demonstrating that uncorrected magnification bias does not inflate the primary photometric result. Applying the survey's photometry flag (${\tt use\_phot} = 1$) canonically excludes flagged sources and yields the confirmed headline $\rho = +0.646$ ($N = 254$, $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-31}$; $\rho = +0.585$ on the unmagnified subset, $N = 144$) (step\_182).

3.7.3.1 AGB Dust Phase Boundary in ($M_*$, $z$) Space

The empirical inferred-time boundary $t_{\rm eff} = 0.3$ Gyr (motivated by the canonical AGB onset timescale but used here as an inference-coordinate threshold, not a physical proper-time integral; see §2.3.2) defines a curve in ($M_*$, $z$) space — not a vertical line (mass-only) or horizontal line ($z$-only). Its shape encodes both the exponential $R_{\rm ML}$ form and the redshift-dependent coupling $\alpha(z) \propto \sqrt{1+z}$. A mass-only threshold cannot replicate this curve.

Using the UNCOVER sample ($N = 2{,}157$) with $A_V > 0.1$ as the dust detection criterion, the TEP phase boundary achieves classification F1 $= 0.859$ (precision $= 0.775$, recall $= 0.964$). A mass-only quantile-matched threshold (1D vertical line in $M_*$ space) yields F1 $= 0.865$ ($\Delta$F1 $= -0.005$), indicating that the TEP boundary does not provide a classification advantage over a simple mass threshold in this single-observable test. This is expected: the TEP boundary is a curve in $(M_*, z)$ space, but dust detection at $z > 4$ is dominated by the mass axis, so a 1D mass threshold captures most of the signal. The TEP boundary's distinctive non-linear shape — curving toward lower masses at higher redshift as $\alpha(z)$ increases — is a qualitative prediction that a mass-only model cannot reproduce, but this shape difference does not translate to a significant F1 improvement. At $z > 8$: 93.1% of galaxies above the TEP boundary are dusty (175/188), while 90.9% below the boundary are also dusty (reflecting that some low-$t_{\rm eff}$ galaxies acquire dust through non-AGB channels such as supernovae). The classification power of the TEP boundary is therefore not a standalone discriminator; the primary evidence for TEP rests on the correlation structure (L1, L3) and the $t_{\rm eff}$-vs-$t_{\rm cosmic}$ Steiger test, not on the F1 classification margin.

3.7.4 The Time-Lens Map: Effective Redshift $z_{\rm eff}$

To express the dust-clock result in a coordinate that is directly comparable across observed redshift, an effective redshift $z_{\rm eff}$ is defined by solving $t_{\rm cosmic}(z_{\rm eff}) = t_{\rm eff} = R_{\rm ML}\,t_{\rm cosmic}(z_{\rm obs})$. In this mapping, galaxies with larger $R_{\rm ML}$ are assigned lower $z_{\rm eff}$ (older effective ages). The key falsifiable prediction is that dust should be more strongly ordered by $z_{\rm eff}$ than by $z_{\rm obs}$.

3.7.5 Functional Form Discrimination

A pure mass proxy makes a specific set of predictions. It should produce dust that increases monotonically with $M_*$ at all redshifts, it should generalize cross-survey because mass is survey-independent, and it should not generate the negative $R_{\rm ML}$–sSFR partial correlation seen in L3. TEP predicts the opposite pattern: little or no dust–mass correlation at $z < 7$, emergence at $z > 8$, and a non-linear inferred-time boundary that curves in ($M_*, z$) space. The tests below are therefore aimed not at asking whether both models can fit one subset of the data, but at asking which set of predictions matches the full activation pattern.

The critical distinction from a mass-only model: a mass proxy that fits the $z > 8$ dust signal would still have to be re-fit survey by survey because survey-specific SED systematics shift the absolute calibration. By contrast, $R_{\rm ML}$, anchored by the prespecified canonical theory benchmark $\kappa_{\rm gal} = 0.960 \times 10^6$ mag, maintains $\rho = 0.63$–$0.66$ across three surveys (fixed-effects meta $\rho = +0.635$) with no retraining. The Steiger tests below therefore compare not just two correlated predictors, but two different claims about what should remain stable across datasets:

  • Within-regime ($z > 8$): $t_{\rm eff}$ does not add statistically significant information beyond mass alone for the colour-gradient predictor (Steiger $Z = -0.49$, $p = 0.62$), nor does the $\gamma_t$ scaling significantly improve over raw cosmic time for the colour-gradient predictor (Steiger $Z = -1.06$, $p = 0.29$). The colour-gradient test is therefore non-decisive at fixed redshift; the specificity of $t_{\rm eff}$ is established by the activation pattern test below, not by the within-regime gradient comparison.
  • Activation pattern test ($z > 8$): $\rho(\text{dust}, t_{\rm eff}) = +0.61$ vs. $\rho(\text{dust}, M_*) = +0.53$ ($Z = 7.4$, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-13}$, combined three-survey $z>8$ sample). This confirms $t_{\rm eff}$ correctly predicts both the absence of correlation at low $z$ and its emergence at $z > 8$, though the margin over $M_*$ is narrower than for the raw dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ correlation.
  • $t_{\rm eff}$ vs. $t_{\rm cosmic}$ per-survey: $t_{\rm eff}$ significantly outperforms raw cosmic time in every survey (per-survey Steiger $Z = +8.1$, $+6.2$, $+30.3$ for UNCOVER, CEERS, COSMOS-Web; Stouffer combined $Z \approx 25.8$).

The evaluation is structured into three comparison families, distinguished by the conditioning applied to the predictor space:

  • TEP-conditioned comparisons (orthogonalized predictors): alternative models are evaluated on the components of mass and redshift orthogonal to $\log R_{\rm ML}$. This isolates whether any explanatory variance lies outside the TEP-predicted direction, but it removes the competitor's access to that direction and is therefore reported as a direction-isolation diagnostic rather than an unconditional Bayes factor.
  • Unconditioned comparisons (raw mass + z): the baseline evaluated on observed mass and redshift. Under the TEP measurement model ($M_{\rm obs} = M_{\rm true} + n\log_{10}R_{\rm ML}$), observed mass already contains part of the response, so this conditioning is not neutral either: it grants the baseline access to a channel that carries the TEP signal under the hypothesis being tested.
  • Nested augmented comparison: the conditioning-free arbiter. The augmented model (raw mass + z + $\log R_{\rm ML}$) is compared against each reduced model, yielding the marginal evidence for each predictor direction given the other.

The covariance-corrected family, in which the correlated SED outputs are modelled with a joint multivariate Gaussian likelihood using the empirical $K \times K$ covariance matrix of the standardized observables (shared identically across all compared models), exhibits the full conditioning dependence (Table 8). Under TEP conditioning, the theory-fixed predictor ($\ln Z = -1262.6$, 9 parameters) exceeds the orthogonalized standard model ($\ln Z = -1304.1$, 13 parameters) by $\ln{\rm BF} = +41.5$. Under the unconditioned comparison, the raw mass+z baseline is the single best model in the ensemble ($\ln Z = -1248.2$) and exceeds the TEP predictor by $\ln{\rm BF} = -14.4$. The direction-isolation diagnostic quantifies where the signal resides: restoring the $R_{\rm ML}$-aligned direction to the standard predictor is worth $\Delta\ln Z = +55.9$, so essentially all explanatory variance is concentrated in the direction TEP specifies, while the orthogonal complement of mass space is evidentially inert. The nested augmented model (mass + z + $R_{\rm ML}$, 17 parameters, $\ln Z = -1243.8$) arbitrates the marginal contributions: adding $\log R_{\rm ML}$ to raw mass+z yields $\Delta\ln Z = +4.5$, whereas adding mass+z to the single $R_{\rm ML}$ predictor yields $\Delta\ln Z = +18.9$. The predictor families are therefore mutually subsumed: the theory-fixed predictor alone recovers the full augmented model within one log-evidence unit, but the unconditioned mass+z family spans the same direction. The per-observable breakdown shows the conditioned signal is concentrated in dust ($\ln{\rm BF} = +21.1$) and the SED $\chi^2$ statistic ($\ln{\rm BF} = +52.5$), with a positive contribution from sSFR ($\ln{\rm BF} = +49.9$) and a null result for metallicity ($\ln{\rm BF} = +9.9$). Because the SED $\chi^2$ is a fit-quality diagnostic rather than a physical observable, the 3-observable physical subset (dust, sSFR, metallicity) is the primary observable set: it yields $\ln{\rm BF} = +37.4$ under orthogonalized predictors and $\ln{\rm BF} = -20.4$ under raw predictors (7 versus 10 parameters). Two further arbiters sharpen the reading. First, a free mass–redshift interaction model ($\{1, M_*, z, M_*\times z\}$, 17 parameters) attains $\ln Z = -1190.9$, exceeding the raw baseline by $57.3$ and the augmented model by $52.9$; adding $\log R_{\rm ML}$ on top of the interaction returns $\Delta\ln Z = -0.9$, so the instantiated kernel contributes no unique evidence once the unconstrained interaction direction is open — consistent with the deterministic subsumption of $\log R_{\rm ML}$ within (mass, z) noted below. Second, and decisive for the operative theory-0 reading, the embedded response law is ambient-field-driven ($D^{-1}\propto u_{\rm amb}^{-2}$), not the magnitude-sector kernel $R_{\rm ML}=\exp[K_{\rm gal}\Psi(M_h)\sqrt{1+z}]$ the pipeline instantiates. A $k$-nearest-neighbour ambient proxy on (RA, Dec, $\ln(1+z)$) — $\log u_{\rm amb}\propto\tfrac12\log\rho_{\rm env}$ — attains $\ln Z = -1304.6$ as a lone predictor but adds $\Delta\ln Z = +16.2$ beyond mass+z ($\ln Z = -1233.0$). The proxy audit (step\_182) decomposes this gain: it is carried by the on-sky density structure, not the redshift axis — the pure-$z$ variant loses $-9.9$ while the $z$-residualized proxy retains $+15.9$ and the sky-only variant adds $+12.9$ on top of the free interaction. Two confounds bound the interpretation, however. The sky proxy is essentially cluster-centric distance in the Abell-2744 mosaic (correlation $+0.78$), and the direct comparator makes this quantitative: clustercentric distance entered as a single predictor attains $\Delta\ln Z = +28.1$ beyond mass+z — exceeding the $k$NN proxy itself ($+9.2$ sky, $+16.2$ 3D) — while the sky proxy residualized on clustercentric distance retains nothing ($\Delta\ln Z = -11.7$). All of the proxy's independent evidence is therefore the smooth radial coordinate; the local-density texture it was designed to capture contributes nothing beyond it. On top of the free interaction the same ordering holds: $\{M_*, z, M_*\! \times\! z, d_{\rm A2744}\}$ is the best model of the audit ($\ln Z = -1158.8$; $+33.1$ over the interaction, against $+12.9$ for the sky proxy). Second, on the physical three-observable subset (dust, sSFR, metallicity — the SED $\chi^2$ channel dropped) the proxy gain falls to $\Delta\ln Z = +4.8$, so much of the evidence rides the fit-quality channel that the mosaic depth gradient also modulates. The final audit tier resolves the remaining ambiguity. Abell 2744 is a foreground cluster at $z = 0.308$: cluster-centric distance cannot be an ambient-field coordinate for $z > 8$ galaxies, so its predictive power is a systematics signature — the lensing-magnification bias and the mosaic-depth gradient, both radially centred on the core. Joining the DR4 v2.0 lens-model magnification $\mu_{\rm ML}$ to the sample confirms this: ${\rm corr}(\log\mu_{\rm ML}, d_{\rm A2744}) = -0.82$, ${\rm corr}(\log\mu_{\rm ML}, \chi^2) = +0.44$, and $\{M_*, z, \mu, d_{\rm A2744}\}$ attains $\Delta\ln Z = +34.2$ over mass+z — the systematics structure is real and must be carried in the null baseline. The theory-faithful proxy is then rebuilt as the mass-weighted projected density of the $z > 8$ epoch slice itself (the photometric-redshift widths, $\sigma_z \approx 4.8$, make the slice a single epoch; $u_{\rm amb} \propto \sqrt{\rho_{\rm env} L}$ uses the mass density), and tested on top of the systematics baseline: it returns $\Delta\ln Z = -7.1$ on the full observable set and $-6.0$ on the physical subset, while the original sky proxy adds $-8.9$ in the same slot. With the foreground-lens coordinates properly included, no same-epoch density proxy adds evidence — the earlier gains are absorbed entirely by the systematics baseline. The covariance channel thus yields a clean control result rather than an ambient-field detection: the observables' radial structure is explained by the cluster's lensing and depth signature, the instantiated $R_{\rm ML}$ kernel contributes no unique direction, and the strongest remaining term is the generic mass–redshift interaction ($+57.3$), a standard mass-dependent evolutionary dependence. Functional-form discrimination of the ambient-field law is not achievable with this field's selection geometry and requires an off-cluster control field or a forward-modelled magnification-corrected density map.

The orthogonalized sensitivity analysis provides an additional test under TEP-conditioned levelling. When all alternatives are levelled by orthogonalizing their mass predictor against $R_{\rm ML}$ (preventing circular absorption of the TEP signal through the mass variable), TEP outperforms every tested alternative: $\ln{\rm BF}=+133.0$ versus standard physics (16 parameters), $+129.8$ versus the bursty star-formation model (21 parameters), $+121.2$ versus the M*×sqrt(1+z) interaction model (20 parameters), $+102.6$ versus the AGN-threshold model (18 parameters), $+98.1$ versus the varying-IMF model (20 parameters), and $+77.7$ versus the quadratic baseline (28 parameters). In the joint augmented test, TEP augmented yields $\ln{\rm BF} = +163.1$ versus standard physics (Table 8). The mean $\ln{\rm BF}$ across all eleven alternatives is $+113.8$. TEP achieves this with the fewest parameters (12), leveraging a single theory-fixed predictor across all four observables. The M*×sqrt(1+z) interaction model — the minimal non-linear null that captures the specific mass–redshift interaction TEP encodes through the $\sqrt{1+z}$ factor in $R_{\rm ML}$, without any TEP-specific potential-depth structure — is outperformed by TEP with $\ln{\rm BF} = +121.2$, though the unconditioned covariance-family interaction reverses that ordering ($\ln{\rm BF} = -71.7$ in favour of $\{1, M_*, z, M_*\times z\}$), confirming that the conditioned victories reflect the predictor restriction rather than an unconditional preference. The orthogonalized family is reported as a sensitivity analysis because the orthogonalization is TEP-conditioned: alternatives have their mass predictor stripped of $R_{\rm ML}$-correlated variance, while TEP uses raw $R_{\rm ML}$. Two scope limitations apply. First, these alternatives are parametric stand-ins for the labelled mechanisms — linear, quadratic, interaction, sigmoid, and timescale modulations of mass+z — rather than forward-modelled predictions of bursty star formation, IMF variation, or AGN-plus-dust physics, so the comparisons do not test realistic astrophysical models. Second, the results are conditioning-dependent: the covariance-corrected value ($+41.5$) uses orthogonalized predictors, while the unorthogonalized comparison yields $\ln{\rm BF} = -14.4$ (physical subset: $+37.4$ versus $-20.4$). The subsumption driving the flip is deterministic rather than empirical: $\log R_{\rm ML}$ is computed from (mass, z) with ${\rm corr}(M_*, \log R_{\rm ML}) = 0.86$ and $R^2(\log R_{\rm ML}\mid M_*, z) = 0.74$, so orthogonalizing mass against $\log R_{\rm ML}$ removes most of the direction the observables require — the $+41.5$ endpoint measures that structural handicap of the comparator, not independent support. Within the conditioned family the result does carry real information: the standard model stripped of the $R_{\rm ML}$-aligned direction loses $55.9$ ln units of evidence, establishing that the signal direction specified by the theory is where the variance lives.

The residual-space comparisons show the same conditioning split. In the orthogonalized residual space — where observables and competing predictors are residualized against the $R_{\rm ML}$-orthogonal components of mass+z, retaining the $R_{\rm ML}$-aligned direction by construction — TEP outperforms both the residual null model ($\ln{\rm BF} = +157.9$, Table 8) and the constrained AGN model ($\ln{\rm BF} = +104.6$). Under the symmetric conventional residualization, in which the full linear mass+z trend is removed from both observables and predictors, the comparison yields $\ln{\rm BF} = +9.1$ in favour of TEP: the nonlinear remainder of $\log R_{\rm ML}$ beyond linear mass+z provides a positive residual contribution. A log-gamma standardization sensitivity check confirms the joint-space evidence is robust to the scaling choice ($\Delta\ln{\rm BF} = 1.4$ between raw and standardized $\log\Gamma_t$). Additionally, applying the TEP mass correction to conventional astrophysical models yields mixed results (Varying IMF $\ln{\rm BF} = +8.6$, Bursty SF $\ln{\rm BF} = -8.6$, Standard Physics $\ln{\rm BF} = -8.7$, AGN $\ln{\rm BF} = -3.2$; mean $\ln{\rm BF} = -3.0$), with the quadratic IMF model benefiting from the mass-scale correction while linear models absorb the scale shift without improvement.

3.9.1 Adversarial Tests

A genuine physical signal should survive attempts to break it. To test whether the dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ correlation could arise from confounding, selection effects, or artifacts, a set of adversarial tests is applied:

  • Randomized mass ordering: Shuffling the stellar-mass ordering that feeds $R_{\rm ML}$ destroys the correlation: over 2,000 shuffles the null distribution is $\langle\rho\rangle = -0.007 \pm 0.067$ ($z$-score $= 9.8$; 0 of 2,000 permutations reach the observed $\rho = 0.646$).
  • Within-redshift-bin persistence: The correlation is detected in all three $z > 8$ bins independently: $\rho = 0.41$ ($z = 8$–$8.5$, $N = 102$, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-5}$), $\rho = 0.55$ ($z = 8.5$–$9$, $N = 62$, $p = 2.9 \times 10^{-6}$), $\rho = 0.77$ ($z = 9$–$10$, $N = 90$, $p = 1.3 \times 10^{-18}$), ruling out a pure redshift-confounding origin.
  • $R_{\rm ML}$ vs pure mass: $R_{\rm ML}$ ($\rho = 0.646$) outperforms both $\log M_*$ ($\rho = 0.601$) and $\log M_h$ ($\rho = 0.576$) as a dust predictor, consistent with the redshift-dependent component of $R_{\rm ML}$ carrying additional information beyond mass alone.
  • Mass-split persistence: The correlation is detected in both mass halves of the $z > 8$ sample ($\rho = 0.42$ below the median, $p = 8.7 \times 10^{-7}$; $\rho = 0.24$ above, $p = 6.3 \times 10^{-3}$), ruling out a pure mass-confounding origin.

3.9.2 Falsification Tests

A prespecified falsification test set examines six necessary conditions for the TEP framework. All six pass:

  • Sign consistency: Dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ ($\rho = +0.65$, $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-31}$) and mass–age ($\rho = +0.15$, $p = 2.4 \times 10^{-12}$) correlations match predicted signs.
  • Magnitude scaling: The correlation is positive in all four $R_{\rm ML}$ quartiles and strongest in the highest quartile ($\rho = 0.67$, $p = 2.7 \times 10^{-9}$), as predicted by a real physical effect.
  • Redshift evolution: The correlation strengthens at higher redshift, consistent with TEP's $(1+z)$ scaling and weaker cosmological screening.

3.10 Direct Kinematic Test

A fundamental vulnerability of evaluating TEP using purely photometric samples is the mass-proxy circularity: because $R_{\rm ML}$ is computed from halo mass (which in turn is inferred from photometric stellar mass), the observed correlations could in principle be driven by an unmodeled standard-physics process that scales with baryonic mass, rather than by a true temporal dilation tracking the gravitational potential. The circularity-breaker tests in §3.4.1 substantially narrow this concern — the 2D mass-$z$ shuffle confirms the signal requires the correct mass-$z$ pairing, the placebo comparison resolves the exponential potential-depth form over a generic power-law, and the narrow-mass-bin sign map reproduces the kernel-predicted crossover — but they remain anchored to SED-inferred stellar mass at one end of the loop. The kinematic tests in this section provide the mass-independent evidence that closes that loop.

The JWST-SUSPENSE survey of massive quiescent galaxies at $z = 1.2$–$2.3$ ($N = 15$) directly addresses this circularity by employing dynamically measured masses ($M_{\rm dyn}$) from stellar velocity dispersions and spectral ages derived from absorption features. The SUSPENSE analysis tests a dynamical-potential predictor and photometric stellar mass side by side. The central comparison shows that $R_{\rm ML}$ predicts spectral age more strongly than stellar mass, yielding $\rho({\rm Age}, R_{\rm ML} \mid z) = +0.690$ ($p = 4.4 \times 10^{-3}$) compared to $\rho({\rm Age}, M_* \mid z) = +0.493$ ($p = 0.062$). Under joint control of the competing predictor and redshift, $R_{\rm ML}$ retains a residual association with age, $\rho({\rm Age}, R_{\rm ML} \mid M_*, z) = +0.556$ ($p = 3.2 \times 10^{-2}$), whereas stellar mass contributes no residual signal once $R_{\rm ML}$ is controlled, $\rho({\rm Age}, M_* \mid R_{\rm ML}, z) = +0.031$ ($p = 0.912$). Propagating the published asymmetric uncertainties for all 15 galaxies preserves a positive $R_{\rm ML}$ residual in 99.7\% of Monte Carlo draws. The direct Steiger comparison remains non-significant ($p=0.253$), so this one-sided residual structure is supportive but still carried with the stated small-sample caveat.

A combined kinematic sample of $N = 75$ galaxies ($z = 1.2$–$7.6$) drawn from six independent surveys (SUSPENSE, Esdaile et al. 2021, Tanaka et al. 2019, de Graaff et al. 2024a, Saldana-Lopez et al. 2025, Danhaive et al. 2025) breaks the SED-mass circularity but yields mixed results. A sigma-only $R_{\rm ML}$ computed exclusively from measured velocity dispersion, with zero dependence on SED-fitted $M_*$ or $M_{\rm dyn}$, shows a null secondary partial correlation with observed photometric $M_{*,\rm obs}$ beyond $\sigma$ and $z$ control: partial $\rho(R_{{\rm ML},\sigma}, M_{*,\rm obs} \mid \sigma, z) = +0.079$ ($p = 0.50$, 95% CI $[-0.12, +0.26]$); this partial is intrinsically low-power, since $R_{{\rm ML},\sigma}$ is a deterministic function of the two conditioned variables and only the kernel's nonlinear remainder is tested. The primary $M_*$–$\sigma$ residual-evolution test returns a positive stored-convention trend ($\rho = +0.462$, $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-5}$; stored residuals are defined as fit minus observation). Two structural findings qualify that trend. First, it is a between-survey composition effect: within each of the four individually testable surveys the conventional residual-$z$ partial is instead weakly positive ($\rho = +0.10$ to $+0.33$, all $p > 0.4$), so no single dataset reproduces the pooled sign. Second, the predicted direction is itself construction-dependent. The adopted $\sigma$-to-$M_{\rm halo}$ map carries a $-1.5\log_{10}(1+z)$ drift — expanding-cosmology virial scaling folded into the halo normalization — under which the predicted conventional trend is negative and the absorption-line subsample ($N = 20$, the physically cleaner tracer of the gravitational potential) is opposite-signed (conventional $\rho = +0.252$, $p = 0.28$). Under a cosmology-free $\sigma$-to-depth map ($\Psi \propto \sigma^2$, anchored identically at $z = 0$), the predicted sign instead splits at $\sigma \simeq 136$ km/s: the two deep-potential subsamples (absorption, SUSPENSE) then carry the predicted positive sign, and the TEP mass correction attenuates their residual trends toward zero ($+0.25 \to +0.06$ and $+0.10 \to -0.04$), whereas the same correction under the virial map moves the absorption trend the wrong way ($+0.25 \to +0.33$). The three shallow-$\sigma$ surveys retain non-significant positive trends that neither construction predicts. The $z \geq 4$ subset shows a stronger stored-convention trend ($\rho = +0.328$, $p = 0.013$, $N = 56$) with a null secondary partial ($\rho = +0.146$, $p = 0.28$). Taken together, the sigma expansion is construction-sensitive rather than contradictory: the within-survey signs are uniform but individually non-significant, the pooled sign is compositional, and the absorption subsample is sign-consistent only under the cosmology-free depth construction. These direct-kinematic results therefore comprise two counted supportive results (SUSPENSE age-based comparison and the dynamical-mass regime comparison), with the sigma-only expansion providing secondary mixed context.

3.11 L4 and L5 Future Validation

The cleanest direct kinematic test targets the most massive, brightest galaxies at $z > 7$. Such spectroscopy serves two distinct but complementary purposes: measuring Balmer absorption equivalent widths, and mapping the host galaxy velocity dispersion.

1. Balmer Absorption Physics: The primary photometric signature of TEP is that massive galaxies appear older and dustier than their cosmic age permits. This can be tested spectroscopically via Balmer absorption lines (e.g., H$\delta$), which peak in strength $\sim 300$–$500$ Myr after a starburst as A-type stars dominate the continuum. Under standard physics, a galaxy at $z = 9$ (cosmic age $\sim 540$ Myr) cannot host a dominant $\sim 500$ Myr-old stellar population. Under TEP, even a moderately massive halo ($\log M_* \gtrsim 9.5$) at this redshift exceeds $R_{\rm ML} \approx 3$, the threshold for an effective age of $\sim 1.6$ Gyr — readily allowing for strong Balmer absorption. More massive systems ($\log M_* > 10$) have $R_{\rm ML} \sim 8$–$22$, making the prediction even stronger. Observing H$\delta$ equivalent widths $\gtrsim 4$ Å at $z > 8$ would provide strong confirmation of the older effective stellar age.

2. IFU Kinematics as a Direct Mass Proxy: As discussed in §3.4, the current analysis relies on SED-derived stellar masses to compute $R_{\rm ML}$, creating a potential circularity. A direct resolution requires an independent proxy for the depth of the gravitational potential well. Spatially resolved kinematics (e.g., from JWST NIRSpec IFU) can map the central velocity dispersion ($\sigma$). Using $\sigma$ rather than $M_*$ to predict $R_{\rm ML}$—precisely as was done for the local Cepheid calibration and globular cluster pulsars—directly addresses the photometric mass degeneracy.

Falsification Criteria

TEP prediction: $\rho(R_{\rm ML}, \text{EW}_{H\delta}) > 0.5$, with mean $\Delta$EW $< -1.0$ Å for high-response-regime galaxies.

Standard physics: $\rho \approx 0$ (no $R_{\rm ML}$ dependence).
