Abstract

JWST has revealed a pattern of high-redshift anomalies—such as extreme star formation efficiencies and unexpected stellar-to-dynamical mass ratios—that appear preferentially in deep gravitational potentials. This work tests whether these tensions can be resolved by relaxing the assumption of universal cosmic time. Under the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP)—a continuously screened two-metric framework—proper time depends on the local environment in unscreened halos. Using a prespecified magnitude-sector benchmark ($\kappa_{\rm gal} = 0.960 \times 10^6$ mag, Paper 11 — where distinct empirical Cepheid-channel recoveries bracket the benchmark with a midpoint of $0.943 \times 10^6$ mag, within $1.8\%$, and Step 62 derives $|\kappa_{P^2}^{\rm pred}(\lambda_0=1)| = 1.196 \times 10^6$ mag from the Cepheid envelope density ratio $\rho_T/\langle\rho\rangle(10\text{ d}) = 8.45 \times 10^5$ at the unit-transport benchmark $\lambda_0 = 1$ with bare coupling $|\beta_A| = 1$ fixed by Paper 0, matching the direct SH0ES $P^2$-carrier fit $(1.191 \pm 0.480) \times 10^6$ mag to $0.5\%$ ($\lambda_0 = 0.995 \pm 0.401$)) applied without any JWST-specific parameter refitting, this framework quantitatively accounts for the leading photometric and kinematic excesses.

To address mass-proxy circularity, the framework is tested against kinematic data using the JWST-SUSPENSE survey ($N=15$) and a broader velocity dispersion ($\sigma$)-based expansion ($N=75$, Step 171). In the SUSPENSE sample, the dynamical mass-to-light inference response ($R_{\rm ML}$) retains residual spectral-age information after controlling for stellar mass and redshift ($\rho = +0.556$, $p = 0.032$), whereas stellar mass loses its predictive power once $R_{\rm ML}$ is controlled ($\rho = +0.031$, $p = 0.91$). In the broader $N=75$ velocity-dispersion expansion, evaluating the purely kinematic predictor $R_{\rm ML,\sigma}^{\rm dir}$ under the Virial-exact depth map $\Psi \propto \sigma^2$ (with zero dependence on SED-fitted $M_*$ or size $R_e$) yields a strong circularity-free partial correlation controlling for redshift: $\rho(R_{\rm ML,\sigma}^{\rm dir}, M_{*,\rm obs} \mid z) = +0.463$ ($p = 2.84 \times 10^{-5}$, $N=75$), strengthening to $\rho = +0.608$ ($p = 8.43 \times 10^{-7}$) on the $N=55$ exact-$M_{\rm dyn}$ subsample and $\rho = +0.575$ ($p = 0.0080$) on the $N=20$ stellar absorption-line subsample. Under the Virial-exact depth map $\Psi \propto \sigma^2$, all five physical subsamples (pooled $N=75$, absorption $N=20$, emission $N=55$, $z \ge 4$ $N=56$, and SUSPENSE $N=15$) agree in residual-$z$ sign (5/5), and holding the local $z=0$ reference at $z_{\rm ref}=0$ ($\Psi(\sigma)\sqrt{1+z}-\Psi_{\rm ref}$) yields sign agreement across all three $N \ge 15$ individual surveys plus the absorption tracer while attenuating the absorption-line residual-$z$ trend by $86.5\%$ ($+0.252 \to +0.034$).

In large-sample photometry ($N = 1{,}254$ across three surveys), dust emergence and apparent evolutionary advance align strongly with potential depth ($\rho = +0.635$ at $z > 8$), organizing more cleanly along an effective-time coordinate than raw cosmic time. A secondary photometric test confirms that $R_{\rm ML}$ carries sSFR information beyond mass and redshift ($\rho(R_{\rm ML}, {\rm sSFR} \mid M_*, z) = -0.62$, $p = 8.2 \times 10^{-29}$), with the negative sign predicted by the TEP measurement equations (${\rm sSFR}_{\rm obs} \propto R_{\rm ML}^{m-n_{\rm SPS}}$ with $m < n_{\rm SPS}$). Furthermore, a nested Bayesian model comparison of the SED observables—utilizing a joint covariance likelihood that accounts for correlated outputs (Steps 176, 179)—shows that the nested augmented model (raw mass + $z$ + $\log R_{\rm ML}$, 17 parameters, $\ln Z = -1243.8$) is the single highest-evidence model in the canonical ensemble, beating the unconditioned raw mass+$z$ baseline ($\ln Z = -1248.2$) by $\Delta\ln Z = +4.45 \pm 1.09$ ($\text{BF} = 85.4 : 1$, $>4\sigma$ positive evidence for adding $\log R_{\rm ML}$ despite the 4-parameter Occam penalty), while the direction-isolation diagnostic confirms that the $R_{\rm ML}$-aligned component carries $\Delta\ln Z = +55.9 \pm 1.0$ ($\ln{\rm BF} = +41.5$ for 9-parameter TEP-only over orthogonalized standard, and $\ln{\rm BF} = +9.07 \pm 0.93$, $\text{BF} = 8{,}720 : 1$ in symmetric conventional residual space). Finally, the lensing and environmental-channel audit (Step 182) demonstrates that explicitly de-magnifying stellar masses by the UNCOVER DR4 lens magnification $\mu_{\rm ml}$ sharpens the $z>8$ dust–$R_{\rm ML}$ correlation from $\rho = +0.646$ to $\rho = +0.656$ ($p = 1.30 \times 10^{-32}$, $N=254$), that the correlation holds in the unmagnified $\mu_{\rm ml} \le 1.05$ outer field at $\rho = +0.603$ ($p = 1.29 \times 10^{-15}$, $N=144$) and strengthens to $\rho = +0.667$ ($p = 1.09 \times 10^{-17}$, $N=127$) across sightlines traversing the inner half of the foreground Abell 2744 cluster well ($z = 0.308$), and that the foreground cluster sightline coordinates $(d_{\rm A2744}, \log_{10}\mu_{\rm ml})$ add $\Delta\ln Z = +26.2$ on the 3 physical observables ($+34.2$ on all 4), consistent with intermediate-path temporal transport along the cluster line of sight (Paper 19), positioning TEP as a coherent and falsifiable organizing framework for high-redshift galaxy evolution.
